Preface
Preface

To the Reader, and to the Two of Them

Two years ago, on the seventeenth of November, one house in our family grew a little quieter and another grew warm. We were told, as families are always told, that we were giving a daughter away. We have since found the arithmetic to be quite wrong. Nothing was given away. A son was added, and a sister came to be loved by one more heart.

There is a love that arrives with music and marigolds, and there is a love that remains when the music has stopped. We saw the first upon the wedding night.

8
Preface

In the two years since, we have watched the second practised daily: in patience, in small and unremarked kindnesses, in the ordinary courage of choosing each other every morning.

What follows is that night as we remember it, set down in six chapters. Facing each photograph we have placed a few lines from the poets who understood these matters better than we. The reader is begged to turn the pages slowly, for some joys are best taken in small sips.

The Family.
November, 2026.

Chapter I.

In which the Evening begins

In which the Evening begins

It is a truth universally acknowledged, at least within our family, that no evening was ever quite so bright as this one. The marigolds hung like small suns, the sparklers wrote their light upon the air, and at the centre of all that brightness stood two people who did not yet know how much they would come to mean to each other.

Of the Sacred Fire, and Seven Promises

Of the Sacred Fire, and Seven Promises

Seven rounds of the sacred fire, and seven promises: to walk together, to grow together, to remain friends when the years grow heavy. The flame has long since gone out. The promises, we are glad to report, are burning still.

The Leave-taking

Of all the hours of that long night, this was the one that undid us. A daughter left the house in which she had grown, and every one of us wept as though something were ending. We were mistaken. Two years on, we understand that she did not leave one home. She came to belong to two.

A Month Hence

Letters

My dear Amrita, my dear Ramendra,

When we placed her hand in yours, Ramendra, we believed we were giving away the most precious thing we had. We were mistaken, and gladly so. We gave nothing away. We gained a son, and our daughter gained a home as full of love as the one she left.

Two years on, our prayers for you have not changed; they have only grown longer. Be gentle with each other on the hard days, laugh together on the easy ones, and remember that you have a home here whose doors are always open.

Visit us more often. The house is quieter than we should like.

With every blessing,
Maa and Papa

Letters

Dear Didi, dear Jija ji,

Let the record show that this little volume was no small undertaking. One pendrive, two folders, and some thirteen hundred photographs, gone through with all the patience of a man reading a very long judgment. Twenty-nine were chosen, and a great many beautiful ones were left out.

I set this down only so that the labour may be duly noted. It was considerable. The love behind it was greater still. You two have shown me what a marriage can be, and I could think of no better way to thank you than to tell your story as carefully as I knew how.

Your devoted younger brother,
Uday

Dear Amrita and Ramendra,

Whatever the hour and whatever the trouble, a plan gone wrong, a long road, or simply a day that wants company, you have only to call, and we shall come. We are always there, and we always shall be.

The same holds true for every happiness. We mean to be present at each anniversary that follows, to raise a glass to the two of you, and to remind you how very much you are loved.

Ever yours,
